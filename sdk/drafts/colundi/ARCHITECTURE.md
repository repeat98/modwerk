# Decision · keep the ColdFire machine as the sequencing reference

23 November 2026 · experimental · reviewed against Modwerk main `5e6e4621b` and COLUNDI `bba621508`.

The artist relayed the maintainer's concerns about CPU overload in existing synths and his suggestions to try DSP synthesis or an input-driven tuned resonator. These are design questions. We will keep the verified sine machine, investigate DSP source admission and timing before porting it, and keep the resonator a separate private FX experiment. The decision can change when an integrated DSP voice demonstrates a net benefit on a busy Part.

The audition uses a generated transient and seeded noise. Musical preference, live-input performance and perceived articulation await an artist listening test. ## Isolated DSP comparison

The private experiment assembled four binaries with dsp_asm and ran them on the repository's dspbench interpreter at 44,100 Hz. Both kernels rotate two rounded 24-bit states with a wide multiply-accumulate. B starts at 0.2 amplitude with unit radius. C starts silent, uses radius r = 10^(-3/(44100 × 0.35)), and injects (1−r)/2 times the input. Solve coefficients from Hz before rendering. In exact arithmetic that input gain bounds the wet magnitude by 0.5 for a full-scale bounded input and zero initial state. Fixed-point stores and a bounded wet/dry mix still need overload tests. Keep feedback below unity, cap decay, and slew or crossfade coefficients on live changes.

Each render used 3,000 blocks of 16 frames, the same synthetic input, scratch clobbering, and split offsets 1, 7 and 15. Outputs were bit-identical across splits for each binary. DSPbench counted the executed instructions. For the branch-free DO bodies we also decoded the assembled words, expanded the executed loop counts and applied the SDK interlock timing model, cross-checking its instruction count against dspbench. Per-block figures include the loop setup and return. They exclude dispatch, control transport, AMP and FX.

| One mono voice to two identical channels | Instructions/block, whole / split | Modeled DSP cycles/block, whole / split |
|---|---:|---:|
| B · oscillator | 226 / 228 | 296 / 304 |
| C · resonator | 274 / 276 | 360 / 368 |

The resonator costs **48 more executed instructions and 64 more modeled cycles per 16-sample block** in these implementations. B has no envelope and the probes use fixed coefficients. These are lower-scope measurements than a playable machine or a full FX. For scale, the repository's SPRING REV benchmark reports 257.50 executed instructions/sample unsplit and 314.19 in the expensive split sweep. Our peaks are 14.25 and 17.25 respectively. Spring includes a complete stereo effect in another harness. A fresh stock sweep beside a complete candidate, on the same image with moving controls, remains open. ColdFire instructions, modeled DSP cycles and elapsed interrupt time are separate quantities. [DSP benchmark](../../../sdk/machines/octatrack/elekloader/dspbench/) · [Cycle model](../../../sdk/cycle-scan/README.md) · [Stock comparator and conditions](../../../sdk/octatrack/modules/bbd/evidence/performance.json)

After the synthetic drum/noise transient, measured tail pitches were 440.037 and 441.007 Hz (positive crossings, samples 1,700–7,499). Low-level rounding affects those estimates. Following a one-second full-scale sine at resonance, the early decay slope gave T60 of 350.39/349.98 ms, and the wet peak stayed below 0.251 full scale. **Tail silence failed**: after three seconds without input the final half-second still peaked at 130/126 signed 24-bit LSB. A weak burst also gave a misleading long decay estimate at 441 Hz as its tail approached the rounding floor. Before an FX proposal, test a bounded silence detector or error-feedback quantizer, coefficient transitions, hot broadband input and dirty-state restart.

## SDK boundary and alternatives

The base already prices DSP source voices, and Analog BD sends a marked control record from ColdFire to a packaged DSP engine before AMP and FX. Site 5 is chainable, but its feature list currently contains only Analog BD. Its controller writes the record on the final segment and communicates a trig flag. That example does not prove that COLUNDI can register an independent source or preserve every sample offset with the same format. A proposed control record needs an owner, validated degree, event offset and articulation, with the previous note intact before the split. Request maintainer review of the smallest package/site declaration before any base change. Do not reuse Analog BD's identity or reservation. [Site registry](../../../sdk/machines/octatrack/dsp-sites.json) · [Analog BD controller](../../../sdk/octatrack/modules/analog-bassdrum/control.c) · [Package architecture](../../../docs/OCTATRACK_PACKAGE_DSP_HOOKS.md)

For tuning, use degree → validated absolute Hz → renderer-specific control. The baseline already encodes the last step as a compile-time Q32 increment. B can derive a rotation pair or a DDS increment; C derives a radius and pole angle. The synthetic 440/441 Hz fixture is sufficient. An independently installable general resonator could later offer a cleared tuning mode. A branded catalogue-specific FX would require naming and data permissions first. Neither needs a runtime dependency on the synth.

A resonant band-pass is a compact alternative to the measured rotating pole pair, with coefficient precision and gain to check at high Q. An integer feedback comb produces a harmonic series, but at 44.1 kHz 100 samples give 441 Hz and 101 give 436.63 Hz. It misses 440 Hz. Fractional delay can bridge that gap, at the cost of a delay buffer per channel and frequency-dependent interpolation loss or phase shift. A bounded bank adds independent modes and can produce inharmonic tails, with state and work growing per mode and a new summing-headroom requirement. Start with one mode. RINGMOD SC is a reference for carrier processing, VIBRATO for fractional interpolation, and the effects guide for silence, stereo and lifecycle tests. [FX guide](../../../docs/module-guides/effects.md)

## Next experiment and decision gate

1. Preserve A as the oracle. Compare first PLAY from a saved project, a front-panel edit and a late RAM edit with the same four-note pattern. Check all split offsets. Optimize the always-pure sine path or skip silent work only after measuring the adapter and comparing PCM.
2. If a reviewed DSP source route exists, compare A and B on the same saved Part, stopped and playing, with one active voice and busy stock neighbors. Measure incremental frame interrupt, CPU meter, skipped renders, DSP per-core price and P/X/Y placement. Physical headroom and the net system saving remain **unmeasured**. Keep the ColdFire machine until a candidate preserves pitch, articulation and stock audio while improving the limiting resource. [Performance procedure](../../../docs/MODULE_PERFORMANCE.md)
3. Keep C outside this machine PR. The next private FX test is a hot-input and tail-silence pass followed by an equal-level listening comparison on percussion and sustained material. A separate FX claim, manifest and PR require an agreed musical scope.
