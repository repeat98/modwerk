# Midiverb testing

Record what you ran, on which build and which unit, and what you saw. "Not tested" is a valid answer; never write a result you did not observe, and keep emulator runs apart from hardware ones.

## Emulator (dsp_host)

- **Program table against the original machine** (`node generate.mjs --mv1 … --mv2 … --fex …`, 25 Nov 2026): every Midiverb, Midiverb II and Midifex program that fits the 15,614-word ring unchanged runs bit-identical to the original machine's model (Brombaugh's semantics), its state exactly; the two output taps read a halved word, so they differ by at most 2 in the 16-bit domain. 24 Midiverb, 5 Midifex and 10 Midiverb II programs have their longest delay line shortened to fit (up to about 4.5 %). Midiverb II's 20 chorus and flange programs are run without their LFO: not handled yet.
- **Engine against the table** (`python3 verify.py`, commit ede56e7b6): bit-exact against a model of the table in the DSP's integer arithmetic for five cases (PROG 0, 9, 17, 21, 23; LEVL 16 to 127; MIX 0, 90 and 127), MIX 0 exact dry. One instance per `dsp_host` run: the gate's synthetic image hands every instance the same allocator buffer. Trig splits are not in the model.

## Performance

`dsp_host`, every program streamed by its index at LEVL 127 and MIX 127, unsplit and split at frames 1 and 7. On the first streaming engine 130 of 205 programs cost at most 400 executed instructions a sample (Midiverb 37, Midiverb II 45, Midifex 48), the dearest 589; the 75 over 400 were left out (generate.mjs OVER_BUDGET). On the owner's MKII (twin `4c7eb79b` of base 0.8.1, 25 Nov 2026) one instance of that engine took about 187 per mille of a DSP core, and `device:test` placed three on T1-T3, where core 1 peaked at 921 per mille, over its 900 limit. So the engine was made cheaper (25 Nov 2026): a unit's first word is now its handler's offset (the handlers 16 words apart from mvqJTAB), so it jumps straight there instead of through a branch table; ST and STN wrap the accumulator in place and fetch the next unit in parallel; END swaps the output taps in place. verify.py stays bit-exact in all five cases, its dearest case 356.8 to 294.2. The dearest of the 130 programs, Midiverb #48, went from 399.7 to 329.2, and 330.9 while it fades out during a trig split (402.9 before). Declared at 332, that let the base admit three instances on core 0 of the owner's MKII, where `device:test` read a peak of 998 per mille, so the declared worst case also sets admission. The 75 left out, measured again: 44 cost at most 400 and came back (owner, 25 Nov 2026: the ceiling 400), 31 cost 401 to 558 and stay out. 174 programs (Midiverb 59, Midiverb II 56, Midifex 59): the dearest, Midiverb #16, costs 397.1, and 395.6 fading out during a split; 398 is declared, the test driver's few instructions included. Against stock, from E-Verb's matched benchmark on the same stock image (`164f3122…`): DARK REV 242.38, SPRING REV 314.19, DJ EQ 330.94 (the dearest): 397.1 is 1.64, 1.26 and 1.20 times those. The DSP package is 671 words.
